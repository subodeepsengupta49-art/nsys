📘 Calculus (Class 10–12)

Introduction

Calculus studies change and accumulation. It begins with limits and continuity, then develops derivatives (instantaneous rate of change) and integrals (accumulated quantity). Calculus is essential for physics, engineering, data science, and advanced mathematics.

Class 10 Topics

  • Basic idea of change: intuitive slope and rate of change from graphs and simple functions.
  • Introduction to limits: understanding approaching values (informal).
  • Simple derivatives (conceptual): slope of a straight line and slope of a curve at a point (graphical understanding).
  • Applications: basic motion problems (speed as rate of change), simple maxima/minima ideas for real-life problems.

Class 11 Topics

  • Limits and continuity: formal definition, one-sided limits, limit laws, continuity of functions.
  • Definition of derivative: difference quotient and limit
    Mathematically: \( f'(x) = \displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h} \)
  • Basic derivative rules: power rule, constant rule, sum/difference rules, product and quotient rules.
  • Simple applications: tangent line, velocity from position, increasing/decreasing tests.

Class 11 Video

Class 12 Topics

  • Advanced derivatives: chain rule, implicit differentiation, higher order derivatives.
  • Applications of derivatives: optimization (max/min), curve sketching, related rates, Newton's method.
  • Integrals: antiderivatives, definite and indefinite integrals, Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
  • Techniques of integration: substitution, integration by parts, partial fractions (intro), numerical approximation.
  • Applications of integrals: area under curve, volumes of revolution, work and accumulation problems.
  • Connections to differential equations: simple separable ODEs and growth/decay models.

Key Formulas

Concept Formula (plain) Formula (LaTeX)
Limit (definition) \(\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L\) \(\displaystyle \lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L\)
Derivative (definition) \(f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\) \(\displaystyle f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\)
Power rule \(\frac{d}{dx}x^n = n x^{n-1}\) \(\displaystyle \frac{d}{dx}x^n = n x^{n-1}\)
Product rule \(\frac{d}{dx}[u v] = u'v + uv'\) \(\displaystyle (uv)' = u'v + uv'\)
Quotient rule \(\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)=\frac{u'v-uv'}{v^2}\) \(\displaystyle \left(\frac{u}{v}\right)'=\frac{u'v-uv'}{v^2}\)
Chain rule \(\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))\cdot g'(x)\) \(\displaystyle (f\circ g)'(x)=f'(g(x))g'(x)\)
Fundamental Theorem (part 1) \(\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt = f(x)\) \(\displaystyle \frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt = f(x)\)
Basic integrals \(\int x^n dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C\) (n≠−1) \(\displaystyle \int x^n dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C\)

Examples and Worked Steps

Example 1 — Derivative (power rule)

Find the derivative of \(f(x)=3x^4 - 5x^2 + 7x - 4\).

Step 1: Apply power rule term-by-term
f'(x) = 3 * 4 x^3 - 5 * 2 x + 7 * 1
f'(x) = 12x^3 - 10x + 7

Example 2 — Chain rule

Find derivative of \(y=\sin(3x^2+1)\).

Step 1: Outer derivative: cos(inner)
Step 2: Multiply by inner derivative: d/dx(3x^2+1)=6x
Result: y' = cos(3x^2+1) * 6x

Example 3 — Definite integral (area)

Compute \(\int_0^2 (2x+1)\,dx\).

Antiderivative: ∫(2x+1) dx = x^2 + x
Evaluate from 0 to 2: (2^2 + 2) - (0 + 0) = 4 + 2 = 6

These examples show the standard steps students should practice: identify rule, apply algebra, simplify, and check units/limits where relevant.
